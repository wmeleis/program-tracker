# Registrar's Office Curriculum Review Guide (Graduate Programs)

*Derived from 2 years of Registrar's Office reviewer comments on graduate program
proposals in CourseLeaf CIM (2024-07 → 2026-07). Source: 2,779 workflow comments
across 516 programs; the rules below are the recurring, policy-bearing patterns.
Registrar's Office authors: Heather Daly (primary), David Rogers (Banner setup),
Mariana De Paula, MJ Boudreault, Benoy Joseph, Christine Cornwall, Melanie Standig,
Melissa Couch-Hrinda, Melissa Daigle-Briggs.*

**How to use this.** These are conventions the Registrar's Office applies during
review that are not fully published elsewhere. Aligning a proposal to them *before*
submission avoids rollbacks and speeds approval. Each rule states the convention
and, where useful, why it exists.

---

## 1. Effective term & catalog edition

**1.1 — The effective catalog edition must match the effective term.** A Fall YYYY
effective term maps to the YYYY–(YYYY+1) catalog edition.

**1.2 — Program-requirement changes take effect on a catalog-year (Fall) basis only.**
A requirement change cannot be effective mid-year (e.g. Spring); the effective term is moved to the next Fall.

**1.3 — There is an annual college-approval deadline per catalog year; miss it and the proposal rolls to the next edition.** The Registrar moves the edition forward when the deadline has passed.

**1.4 — New degree programs needing Faculty Senate / Board approval may be pushed to a later term/edition**, but can be included in a catalog *addendum* if fully approved before publication.

---

## 2. Program title & naming

**2.1 — Campus-specific program records must carry the campus in the full program title, in parentheses.** Per the Registrar's "campus-specific records project."

**2.2 — Degree and campus are both reflected in the full program title.**

**2.3 — Minors do NOT carry a campus parenthetical.**

**2.4 — Online program titles follow the standard "(Online)" convention.** The special "(Online – Vancouver Requirements)" form is a one-off regulatory case and should **not** be used as a model for new online programs.

---

## 3. Program overview language

**3.1 — Remove implied promises of student achievement / outcomes.**

**3.2 — Do not imply the curriculum includes remedial or undergraduate coursework.**

**3.3 — Overview must state whether a concentration is optional or required, consistently with the requirements.** If optional, say "optional"; if a single mandatory concentration, integrate it into the text.

**3.4 — Overview links for multi-campus programs must point to the network-wide listing, not a single campus.**

---

## 4. Concentrations

**4.1 — If a concentration is not required to complete the program, the heading is not "Concentrations."** It becomes "Concentration or Electives Option," and instructions like "Complete one of the following concentrations" are removed.

**4.2 — The internal-use "Concentration(s)" field must be populated**, including **NOCC (No Concentration)** when none is required, and the "Can students choose this concentration at the time of admission?" flag set consistently across the program's concentrations.

**4.3 — Inactive courses inside a concentration must be resolved before approval** (proposal is routed back to the college dean's office to fix).

**4.4 — Concentration requirements should be consistent across a program's campus deployments** (align non-Boston records to the Boston record).

---

## 5. Credit hours

**5.1 — The catalog publishes only the MINIMUM hours required to complete the program** — ranges like "34–35 SH" become "34 SH". Exception: Align/Bridge/Connect programs, which may legitimately require more.

**5.2 — Restricted-elective / choice language uses "at least N SH" / "a minimum of N SH," not a range.**

**5.3 — Remove "maximum of N semester hours" statements.** The catalog documents the minimum for completion; students may exceed it where options allow.

**5.4 — Credit hours must be semester hours (SH), not quarter hours (QH).** QH references are corrected (part of the CPS semester conversion).

**5.5 — For "choose N SH from the following" requirements, put the hours in the dedicated hours field and indent the options** so credit is associated with the requirement, not shown per-option. Conversely, **core-requirement courses are un-indented** so their hours display.

---

## 6. Committee routing (UGCC / UUCC / Graduate Council)

**6.1 — Any change to core requirements must be routed to UGCC (graduate).**

**6.2 — Adding courses from other colleges (or adding/removing a college's course from a UIP) requires UGCC review.**

**6.3 — A "Course Not Found" error in a curriculum clears once the referenced new course is approved by UGCC** — it is not a defect to fix in the program proposal.

**6.4 — Online programs do not require GRA review** and are routed to the Registrar's degree-audit team instead.

---

## 7. Admission, inactivation & teach-out

**7.1 — Set "Is this program open for students applying through admissions?" correctly** — but note PlusOne students are admitted via Slate, so a program admitting PlusOne students must remain "Yes" even if not open to external applicants.

**7.2 — An inactivation proposal is held until its successor new-program proposal is ready; both advance to the same UGCC meeting.**

**7.3 — The "Final Catalog" field on an inactivation must match the inactivation's effective term.** Inactivated as of Fall 2025 → final catalog 2024-2025 (not 2025-2026).

---

## 8. Degree audit, CIP codes & campus/modality

**8.1 — CIP code and campus/modality fields are set by the Registrar's degree-audit team** on new program records (an administrative step, not a college action).

**8.2 — An online program flipping BOS → Online (VTL) for 2026-2027 is not a genuinely new program**, even though CourseLeaf tags it "New Program Proposal" — it's the creation of the VTL-campus record. Registrar adds the Banner program/major code and existing CIP.

**8.3 — Dual-degree programs have no degree-audit impact** and skip that step.

**8.4 — Credit-bearing experiential-learning courses that also satisfy an elective must be listed in the electives list** for degree audits to function.

---

## 9. Grade / GPA requirements

**9.1 — Minimum-grade statements at core requirements use the college's standard wording.** For COE: "The College of Engineering requires that students must earn a grade of C or higher in all required core courses…" — added/normalized per college request.

**9.2 — Prerequisites outside the program should not be required for core courses.** The minimum-grade statement is simplified to "A grade of C or higher is required for each of the following courses."

**9.3 — Don't add "prerequisite conditions may require a higher minimum grade" when the program has only one core course** (the sentence is dropped as inapplicable).

---

## 10. Prerequisites

**10.1 — When elective options require prerequisites outside the program, add the standard alert language** and ensure there are completion pathways that don't require the external coursework.

---

## 11. Experiential learning / co-op

**11.1 — If co-op/experiential participation adds hours or tuition, the Program Requirements must alert students.**

**11.2 — Optional experiential learning is placed in a dedicated "Optional Experiential Learning Opportunities" area** (added with college authorization).

**11.3 — Non-credit / non-existent co-op prep courses are removed from the curriculum.** A line is deleted if the course was never created or awards no credit.

---

## 12. Thesis / capstone

**12.1 — Zero-credit special-purpose courses (Thesis Continuation, Research) are removed from elective lists** — they don't award credit toward the elective hours.

**12.2 — A heading offering "Thesis" must reflect what is actually offered** — e.g. "Capstone or Thesis" → "Capstone or Project" when thesis is no longer an option.

**12.3 — A thesis sequence that relies on a repeatable course requires that course to be UGCC-approved as repeatable first.**

---

## 13. Dual / joint degrees

**13.1 — Dual-degree programs have no standalone degree-audit impact** (see 8.3) but **changes to shared credits still go to UGCC.**

**13.2 — Dual-degree catalog URLs get a redirect** established by the Registrar when program pages move.

---

## 14. Curriculum-list hygiene

**14.1 — No duplicate courses in a list; electives are kept in alphanumeric order.**

**14.2 — Inactive ("red-boxed") courses must be resolved by the college before approval;** the Registrar removes inactive courses from requirement options.

**14.3 — Lines for courses that don't exist are removed** (phantom courses that were never created).
